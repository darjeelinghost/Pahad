PAHAD is intended to be a lasting, independent community institution. Its governance framework is designed to protect its mission, uphold responsible decision-making and ensure that institutional authority is exercised transparently and in accordance with its Constitution and applicable law.

CONSTITUTIONAL INTEGRITY · RESPONSIBLE STEWARDSHIP · PUBLIC ACCOUNTABILITY

OUR GOVERNANCE APPROACH

Institutions must be stronger than individuals.

Good governance is not simply an organisational chart. It is the system through which purpose is protected, authority is defined, decisions are made, resources are safeguarded and people can be held accountable.

PAHAD's governance approach seeks to balance founding vision with collective institutional responsibility. It recognises the importance of constitutional membership, lawful governing authority, clear executive responsibilities and transparent public accountability. No individual, office or programme should operate outside the institution's governing framework.

PAHAD will develop its structures progressively, in line with its legal form, registered Constitution, actual capacity and community priorities.

INSTITUTIONAL FRAMEWORK

Distinct responsibilities.
One shared mandate.

The following describes PAHAD's intended governance architecture. The precise composition, powers, procedures and office-bearer arrangements must be governed by the registered Constitution and duly adopted regulations.

01 / CONSTITUTIONAL MEMBERSHIP

General Body

The General Body represents the institution's membership and provides constitutional oversight through the powers assigned to it by the Constitution and applicable law. Its responsibilities may include receiving annual reports and audited accounts, reviewing institutional performance, electing or appointing office-bearers where prescribed, and considering constitutional amendments and other reserved matters.

02 / GOVERNING AUTHORITY

Governing Body

The Governing Body is intended to provide formal institutional direction and oversight, discharge the duties assigned to it under the Constitution, and act in the best interests of PAHAD. It is responsible for ensuring lawful administration, financial stewardship, policy implementation and proper supervision of the institution's affairs.

03 / FOUNDING STEWARDSHIP

Founder-Patron

The Founder-Patron role is intended to preserve PAHAD's founding purpose, long-term vision and institutional continuity. Any reserved or protective responsibilities must be expressly recognised in the Constitution or validly adopted governing instruments, and remain subject to applicable law, members' lawful rights and the statutory responsibilities of the competent bodies.

04 / EXECUTIVE COORDINATION

Executive Leadership & Secretariat

Executive leadership and the Secretariat are intended to coordinate day-to-day administration, programme delivery, records, communications and implementation of approved decisions. Their authority should be delegated, documented and accountable to the appropriate governing authority.

05 / PROGRAMME DELIVERY

Directorates & Institutional Units

PAHAD may develop directorates, departments, project teams and specialist units as its work expands. These structures should be established only when needed, with approved terms of reference, defined reporting lines, budgets and performance responsibilities.

06 / ADVICE & PARTICIPATION

Advisory and Consultative Platforms

Advisory groups, youth platforms, expert panels and community consultations may support participation and informed decision-making. Unless formally authorised by the Constitution or a valid delegation, advisory bodies do not replace the powers or duties of the General Body or Governing Body.

GOVERNING PRINCIPLES

Authority must always be matched by responsibility.

PAHAD's institutional culture is intended to be guided by the following principles.

Constitutional supremacy

Institutional decisions and office-holder powers must remain within the Constitution, valid regulations and applicable law.

Collective responsibility

Major institutional decisions should follow defined procedures, recorded deliberation and appropriate oversight.

Mission protection

PAHAD's independence, non-partisan character and public-benefit purpose should be protected across leadership changes.

Separation of duties

Governance, executive administration, programme implementation and financial control should have clear and proportionate boundaries.

Participation and inclusion

Members and communities should have meaningful opportunities to contribute through lawful and accessible processes.

Transparency and integrity

Decisions, finances and institutional performance should be documented and disclosed appropriately.

INTEGRITY & SAFEGUARDS

Trust is built through systems.

PAHAD intends to establish proportionate safeguards as its operations develop. These safeguards should be reflected in approved policies, financial procedures and governing records, rather than existing only as statements of principle.

  • Proper minutes, resolutions, registers and institutional records.
  • Clear financial authorisation, documented expenditure and appropriate dual controls.
  • Annual accounts, independent audit where required, and review of audit observations.
  • Disclosure and management of actual, potential or perceived conflicts of interest.
  • Fair procedures for complaints, grievances, misconduct and internal disputes.
  • Clear rules for vacancies, succession, temporary inactivity and continuity of essential functions.
  • Protection and proper use of institutional funds, property, data and intellectual assets.
  • Periodic review of programmes, risks, policies and organisational performance.

INSTITUTIONAL CONTINUITY

Built to evolve.
Designed to endure.

PAHAD's long-term development may involve new programmes, specialist institutions, research or educational initiatives, partnerships and separately governed enterprises. Growth should strengthen—not blur—the institution's public-benefit mandate.

Continuity beyond leadership

Institutional knowledge, records, assets and responsibilities should remain with PAHAD and be protected through lawful succession, handover and caretaker arrangements.

Responsible institutional growth

New bodies, units or legal entities should be created only after assessing their purpose, legal form, governance, resources and relationship with PAHAD.

Separation of public-benefit and commercial activity

Where commercial or enterprise activities are developed, they should have appropriate legal and governance separation, distinct accounts and documented agreements. PAHAD's assets must not be treated as private property.

Change through due process

Constitutional amendments, restructuring and major changes should follow the procedures prescribed by the Constitution and applicable law, with proper records and member participation where required.
